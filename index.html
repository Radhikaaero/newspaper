SDG 13 · 11 · 1 · Top story

Akvan Strikes: 3 Lakh Moved to Safety Before the Storm

GAIA HISTORY

On 29 October 1999 a super cyclone killed around 10,000 people in Odisha. The state then became the first in India to set up its own disaster management authority and built more than 800 multi-purpose cyclone shelters. Before Cyclone Phailin in 2013, close to 1 million people were evacuated.

GAIA NOW

Severe cyclonic storm Akvan crossed the north Andhra Pradesh and south Odisha coast near Gopalpur on Friday, 20 November, bringing strong winds and heavy rain to Odisha, Andhra Pradesh, Jharkhand and parts of West Bengal. Before landfall, more than three lakh people were evacuated in Odisha and 69 trains were cancelled, according to PTI. Red alerts covered 11 districts.

GAIA FUTURE

Damage and relief assessments are still under way. The real test of Odisha’s approach is how many lives were saved, and we will report the figures in the next edition.

SDG angle: Early warnings and evacuations (Goal 13) protect people in coastal communities (Goal 11), and the poorest lose the most when storms hit (Goal 1).

SDG 3 · 2

Made in India, Made for Chickens: First Home-Grown Bird Flu Shot

GAIA HISTORY

India confirmed its first outbreak of highly pathogenic H5N1 bird flu in poultry on 18 February 2006, at farms in Navapur, Maharashtra. Mass culling of birds followed.

GAIA NOW

The Union Agriculture Minister was due to dedicate India’s first indigenous inactivated H5N1 vaccine for chickens on 20 November. It was developed by ICAR-NIHSAD in Bhopal. The launch falls in World AMR Awareness Week (18 to 24 November), whose theme, One Health, One Action, links human, animal and environmental health.

GAIA FUTURE

The launch is the first step. Farmers and vets will now look for details on when and how the vaccine reaches farms, which the summaries we found did not cover.

SDG angle: Healthy poultry protect food supply and rural incomes (Goal 2) and reduce disease risks for people (Goal 3).

SDG 7 · 13

A Fifth of India’s Green Power Now Blows In on the Wind

GAIA HISTORY

Wind turbines first appeared in India in the 1980s, and installations gathered pace from 2005. In 2014 India had about 21 GW of wind capacity.

GAIA NOW

A PIB backgrounder released on 18 November says India’s installed wind capacity stood at 59.20 GW on 30 September 2026. That is about 19.8% of the country’s total installed renewable capacity of 299.25 GW.

GAIA FUTURE

The government aims for 100 GW of wind capacity by 2030 and 156 GW by 2036, as part of its commitment to 500 GW of non-fossil electricity capacity by 2030.

SDG angle: Affordable, clean energy (Goal 7) is how India can cut emissions (Goal 13) while meeting rising power demand.

Sources: PIB document, June 2026 ↗ · CEEW ↗ · Business Standard ↗ · Current Affairs summary of PIB, 19 Nov ↗

International

SDG 13 · 11 · 12 · 14 · 15

Bhutan Writes History at Mumbai’s Earthshot Night

GAIA HISTORY

Prince William launched the Earthshot Prize in 2020. Every year from 2021 to 2030 it awards five £1 million prizes, aiming to back at least 50 solutions to environmental problems by 2030.

GAIA NOW

The sixth Earthshot Prize awards night was held in Mumbai on 17 November, and each of the five winners receives £1 million. The winners are Mahindra Last Mile Mobility (India, clean air, with more than 400,000 electric three-wheelers and small commercial vehicles sold), Bhutan (nature, the first country ever to win, for protecting forests that safeguard Himalayan water systems), Niue Ocean Wide Trust (oceans), PFAS-Free France (a waste-free world) and Ørsted (climate, through offshore wind).

GAIA FUTURE

Winners now have to scale up. Bhutan plans to restore 50,000 hectares of damaged land by 2030, and Mahindra aims to reach 1 million vehicles by 2030.

SDG angle: One evening, five solutions that touch cities (Goal 11), responsible production (Goal 12), oceans (Goal 14) and land (Goal 15).

Sources: Earthshot Prize, launch ↗ · Business Standard, 17 Nov ↗ · AP via WRAL ↗ · Nature World News, 19 Nov ↗

SDG 13 · 17

COP31 Overtime Drama: Fossil Fuels Vanish From the Draft

GAIA HISTORY

At COP29, richer countries agreed to mobilise $300 billion a year by 2035 for developing countries, within a wider $1.3 trillion goal. Developing countries have come to Antalya asking for a clear path to that money.

GAIA NOW

The summit was due to end on 20 November, but nearly 200 countries failed to agree a final deal and talks spilled into overtime. The latest draft, which COP31 President Murat Kurum calls the Antalya Declaration, dropped all references to transitioning away from fossil fuels and a proposed $120 billion adaptation finance target. Norway says the final text must keep language agreed at earlier summits. India’s Environment Minister told the summit that climate action must rest on equity and climate justice.

GAIA FUTURE

A third draft was expected on Saturday morning. Nothing is final until nearly 200 countries agree, so watch for the closing text and whether fossil fuels return to it. This story is still developing.

SDG angle: Climate action (Goal 13) depends on partnerships (Goal 17), and this week the sticking point is who commits to what.

Sources: EUalive, 11 Nov ↗ · RTÉ, 19 Nov ↗ · World Today Journal, 20 Nov ↗ · Climate Action ↗

SDG 6 · 11 · 16

Foul River, Hefty Fines: Supreme Court Puts States on Notice

GAIA HISTORY

The Yamuna Action Plan began in 1993 with Japanese assistance. In its first two phases (1993 to 2013) about Rs 1,514 crore went into sewage treatment plants and sewer lines, yet most of the river’s 22-km stretch in Delhi remains ecologically dead.

GAIA NOW

In an order dated 4 November and made public on 16 November, a Supreme Court bench of Justices Manoj Misra and Manmohan warned states and Union Territories along the Yamuna of financial penalties if the river’s water quality worsens as it flows through their areas. The court said pollution has increased despite huge spending, and blamed weak civic administration. It told the Central Pollution Control Board to monitor water quality where the river and its major drains enter and leave each state, Union Territory and district.

GAIA FUTURE

The case returns to the Supreme Court on 13 January 2027. By then every state along the river knows that a worsening stretch can now cost it money.

SDG angle: Clean water (Goal 6) in cities (Goal 11) needs accountable institutions (Goal 16), and this order says who is responsible.

Sources: ThePrint ↗ · Business Standard, 17 Nov ↗ · Ideas for India ↗ · Business Standard, Mar 2025 ↗

Bengaluru

SDG 11 · 6 · 13 · 15 · Bengaluru

Bengaluru Gets Climate Plans, Ward by Ward

GAIA HISTORY

Bengaluru was once known as the Garden City for its tree-lined avenues and parks. Its founder, Kempe Gowda I, sanctioned lakes inside the city as a main source of water, which became the interlocking lake system that shaped the city.

GAIA NOW

The Greater Bengaluru Authority has prepared ward-level Climate Action Plans for five pilot wards: Shantinagar, Jakkur, Begur, Vijinapura and Nalwadi Krishnaraja Wadiyar. They cover drainage and stormwater, rainwater harvesting, urban greening and heat, with measures such as rain gardens, cool paints and cool shelters under consideration. The plans are now awaiting budget approval from the city corporations.

GAIA FUTURE

The city’s wider climate plan aims for 10% more tree canopy by 2030 and 20% by 2040, and for green and permeable surfaces to cover 40% of the city by 2040. The five-ward pilot is meant as a model for other wards, and its success will depend on funding and on how well the work is monitored.

SDG angle: Climate-ready cities (Goal 11) need water management (Goal 6), climate action (Goal 13) and trees (Goal 15) working together.

Karnataka

SDG 15 · 11 · 1 · Karnataka

Hassan’s Elephant Crisis: Farmers Want a Permanent Fix

GAIA HISTORY

Karnataka has tried to manage elephant conflict before. An earlier government set up an elephant task force for Hassan, Chikkamagaluru, Mysuru and Kodagu, radio-collared 23 elephants and doubled compensation for deaths from elephant attacks to Rs 15 lakh.

GAIA NOW

A Forest Department operation to drive about 56 wild elephants out of residential areas in Hassan district entered its third day on 19 November. Teams with trained elephants are trying to capture two male elephants suspected of killing two people in three days. On the same day a farmer was killed by an elephant near the Bhadra Reserve Forest in Chikkamagaluru, and another farmer by a bison in Mandya. A trained elephant also died in an attack at the Cauvery Trained Elephant Camp in Mysuru district.

GAIA FUTURE

The department plans to relocate the 56 elephants from Hassan to Bhadra Wildlife Sanctuary, but the plan faces public opposition, and villagers and farmers’ groups are demanding a permanent solution. Reports say shrinking habitat and encroachment are pushing animals towards fields.

SDG angle: Protecting wildlife (Goal 15) and protecting farmers’ lives and livelihoods (Goals 1, 2 and 11) have to be solved together.

FLORA · SDG 15 Life on Land

Mangroves Are Back: Can Salt-Water Forests Keep Winning?

GAIA HISTORY

Mangroves are salt-tolerant coastal trees and shrubs. They store carbon, shelter thousands of species and shield coasts from storms and erosion. For decades their forests shrank.

GAIA NOW

A new global assessment finds mangrove cover is growing again, a modest but significant turnaround. An Indian restoration scientist credits policy, engineering and community action, and says India scaled community-led projects that bring in women.

GAIA FUTURE

Many mass-planting projects have failed. Experts back community-based restoration that respects mangrove ecology instead of chasing tree-planting quotas. Oil pollution and erosion still drive losses in places such as Nigeria’s Niger Delta.

Sources: Eco-Business ↗ · Mongabay mangrove news ↗

Born on Camera: A Rhino Calf and a Species’ Second Chance

GAIA HISTORY

Greater one-horned rhinos once roamed the whole northern Indian subcontinent. Poaching for horns and loss of habitat cut their numbers so far that, at one point, fewer than 200 were left in the wild, according to Chester Zoo’s keepers.

GAIA NOW

CCTV at Chester Zoo in England caught the birth of a calf on 11 October 2026, after a pregnancy of about 15 months. Mother and calf are healthy, and the news spread this week.

GAIA FUTURE

The calf is part of a European conservation breeding programme that aims to keep the species’ gene pool diverse.

SPACE · SDG 9 · 11 · 17

We Nudged an Asteroid. Now Europe Is Inspecting the Dent.

Words to know

  • Asteroid: a rocky leftover from the early days of our solar system.
  • Didymos and Dimorphos: a pair of asteroids. Didymos is about 780 metres across, and it has a small moon, Dimorphos, about 170 metres wide, circling it.
  • DART: a NASA spacecraft that crashed into Dimorphos on purpose in 2022, to test whether people can change an asteroid’s path.
  • Hera: Europe’s spacecraft, about the size of a small car, sent to inspect the crash site.
GAIA HISTORY

On 26 September 2022, NASA’s DART spacecraft hit Dimorphos at about 6 km every second. Dimorphos used to circle Didymos once every 11 hours and 55 minutes. Scientists expected the crash to shorten that by about 7 minutes. A research paper in the journal Nature measured the real change: 33 minutes shorter. The surprise was the rubble thrown out by the crash, which gave the asteroid an extra push.

GAIA NOW

ESA’s Hera spacecraft reached the Didymos pair in early November 2026, two years after it launched. Its first close-up pictures show boulders, craters, grooves and a ridge around the middle of Didymos. Didymos spins once every 2.2 hours or so, and scientists think this may drag loose material towards its middle. Hera is looking at a different side from the one DART saw. Detailed pictures of both asteroids are due on 25 November 2026.

GAIA FUTURE

Over the coming months Hera will study the crash site on Dimorphos close up, make 3D maps of both asteroids and release two small CubeSat spacecraft for extra measurements. This will show how big the dent is and how the surface changed. That helps scientists judge how well this method could protect Earth one day, though it would need years of warning to work.

SDG angle: Planning for a rare but serious disaster (Goal 11) takes new ideas (Goal 9) and countries working together, here NASA and ESA (Goal 17).

STARTUP OF THE WEEK · SDG 9 · 12 · 13 · Bengaluru

From Farm Waste to Factory Ingredients: Bengaluru’s altM Raises ₹97.5 Crore

Startup snapshot

  • Name: altM (Alt Material Innovations)
  • Based in: Bengaluru, Karnataka
  • Founded: 2022, by former Tesla manufacturing leaders Apoorv Garg and Yugal Raj Jain
  • In simple words: altM takes leftover plant waste from farms, such as sugarcane bagasse (the dry fibre left after the juice is taken out) and rice straw, and turns it into ingredients that companies use in their products. Many of these ingredients normally come from fossil sources.
GAIA HISTORY

altM was founded in 2022 by Apoorv Garg and Yugal Raj Jain. It raised a $3.5 million seed round in 2023 and then built a research and pilot manufacturing facility in Bengaluru, putting laboratory work, demonstration manufacturing and pilot production under one roof.

GAIA NOW

On 5 November 2026 altM announced a $10.3 million (about ₹97.5 crore) Series A round, led by Rainmatter by Zerodha and Mela Ventures. Its 70-tonne-a-year pilot plant in Electronic City is installed. Reports say nearly 30 beauty and personal-care brands are already customers and 16 to 18 more are testing its products.

GAIA FUTURE

The money will go to manufacturing capacity, research, customer testing and hiring, as altM moves from pilot to commercial production. Reports say it plans a 1,000-tonne-a-year commercial plant by 2028, and then aims to scale to 10,000 tonnes.

GAIA VIEW

Using farm waste as a raw material could reduce the need for fossil-based chemicals. But altM is still early in the move from pilot to commercial scale. The real test is whether it can match fossil-based ingredients on price and performance at much larger volumes.

SDG angle: New industrial ideas (Goal 9) that turn waste into inputs support responsible production (Goal 12) and climate action (Goal 13).
