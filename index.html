National

SDG 3 · 11 · 13

Half the staff at home: Gurugram fights smog with work-from-home orders

From 5 to 20 November, Gurugram’s administration has told offices to keep no more than half their staff on site and has restricted dust-generating construction and demolition, including roads, flyovers and pipelines. The orders follow directions from the Commission for Air Quality Management. A senior doctor at Medanta warned that severe air is especially risky for children, older people and anyone with heart or lung disease.

SDG angle: Clean air links good health (Goal 3) with sustainable cities (Goal 11) and climate action (Goal 13).

Source: Republic World, 4 Nov ↗

SDG 15 · Life on Land

Can 27,000 orchids be saved from a bulldozer? Kerala just tried.

Researchers in Kerala rescued more than 27,000 tree-dwelling orchids from trees felled or marked for felling for road building, and replanted them in two public wetlands and on a beach. After one to two years, nearly three-quarters were still alive, including many species found only in this region. The team calls rescue a last resort, when the trees cannot be kept.

SDG angle: Roads and development (Goal 9) can be built while protecting plants and habitats (Goal 15).

Source: Mongabay, 10 Nov ↗

International

SDG 13 · 17

COP31 is in its second week. Who will pay the climate bill?

The UN climate summit in Antalya, Türkiye, runs from 9 to 20 November, with Türkiye hosting and Australia guiding the negotiations. Behind the speeches, negotiators from nearly 200 parties are locked in tough talks on climate finance. Developing countries want a clear path to the $300 billion a year that richer nations agreed to mobilise by 2035, and progress towards a wider $1.3 trillion goal.

SDG angle: Climate action (Goal 13) depends on partnerships and money (Goal 17).

Source: EUalive, 11 Nov ↗

SDG 7 · 13

Can the world run on 35% electricity by 2035?

Host Türkiye wants countries to back a goal of lifting electricity’s share of global final energy use from about 21% today to 35% by 2035, replacing direct fossil fuel use in transport, buildings and industry. The UN climate chief framed the choice as “electric or shock”, pointing to price swings in oil, gas and fertiliser, including after the temporary closure of the Strait of Hormuz.

SDG angle: Affordable, clean energy (Goal 7) is the route to lower emissions and steadier household costs.

Source: EUalive, 11 Nov ↗

SDG 2 · Zero Hunger

A record El Niño is coming. Who will protect the farmers who feed us?

Smallholder farmers around the world are bracing for a historically strong El Niño, forecast to peak in December with effects lasting into 2027. As reported by Mongabay, the UN has warned it could push an additional 49 million people into acute hunger, with southern Africa and Central America likely hardest hit. Experts urge early warnings and long-term support for small farms.

SDG angle: Ending hunger (Goal 2) now depends on climate-ready farming (Goal 13).

Source: Mongabay, 12 Nov ↗

SDG 17 · 13

Empty chairs at COP31: which leaders stayed home?

Leaders and senior officials from roughly 100 countries reached Antalya for the high-level segment. According to EUalive, leaders of Germany, France, Italy, Denmark, Ireland, the Netherlands, Canada and Japan stayed away, and the United States is absent after its withdrawal from the Paris Agreement process. Climate Action Network gave its Fossil of the Day award to the absent leaders of wealthy countries, while the British prime minister is among the few G7 leaders attending in person.

SDG angle: Partnerships for the Goals (Goal 17) need political leaders in the room, not only negotiators.

Source: EUalive, 11 Nov ↗

SDG 15 · 12

Bulgaria’s parliament votes to end fur farming

Bulgaria’s parliament has banned breeding animals for valuable fur. Mink, chinchillas, foxes, raccoon dogs and other animals will no longer be raised and killed in the country solely for their pelts.

SDG angle: Laws like this shape responsible production (Goal 12) and the way we treat wildlife and animals (Goal 15).

Source: EUalive ↗

FLORA · SDG 15 Life on Land

Can a rainforest keep dying for three years after the drought ends?

  • History: In the 2015–16 El Niño, extreme drought and fires in Brazil’s Lower Tapajos killed around 2.5 billion trees and plants, a long-term study found. Trees kept dying faster than normal for up to three years afterwards.
  • In the news now: NASA reports that the 2026 El Niño is fuelling wildfires across the tropics, with widespread burning in the Amazon and scientists warning the worst may still be ahead.
  • What next: The El Niño is forecast to peak in December. Researchers urge people to avoid burning this year, because forests scarred by earlier fires are the most exposed.

Sources: ScienceDaily / NASA ↗ · University of Oxford ↗ · Mongabay ↗

FAUNA · SDG 15 Life on Land

Is the “Asian unicorn” hiding in the mist, or already gone?

  • History: Scientists described the saola, a wild ox of the Annamite Mountains, only in 1992. Its secretive nature earned it the nickname Asian unicorn.
  • In the news now: No scientist has recorded one since 2013. The search team in Laos is funded month to month, and zoo donations covered October, but long-term money is unresolved.
  • What next: Teams use sniffer dogs and a DNA kit that can confirm within two hours whether dung, hair or chewed leaves came from a saola. Finding wild animals is the first step towards a possible captive-breeding programme.

Sources: Travelbinger, 11 Nov ↗ · Mongabay ↗ · SEAsia ↗
